Research

In progress · Poundo Robot · started Oct 2026

Can a robot learn to make food from human video, and adapt to uncertain food properties like poundo’s consistency, without a force sensor?

First, what’s poundo?

Pounded yam is a smooth, stretchy West African staple, eaten with soups like egusi. It used to be pounded by hand in a mortar. Today most people make poundo from yam flour: stirred into hot water, then turned hard with a wooden stick until smooth.

That turning is hot, heavy, repetitive work. That’s why I’m teaching a robot to do it.

Photos: Toludpilgrim, CC BY-SA 3.0; Koutchika Lihouenou Gaspard (amala, a yam-flour swallow), CC BY-SA 4.0, cropped; via Wikimedia Commons.

  1. 01

    Learn from video

    The motion comes from clips of people turning poundo. The stick-tip path is tracked and retargeted to a robot arm.

  2. 02

    Handle uncertainty

    The dough goes from loose flour and water to a thick, sticky mass, and no two pots behave the same. The robot has to adapt as the resistance changes.

  3. 03

    No force sensor

    The robot that runs has none. A load-cell stick is used only in training, to measure what a real turn takes.
