# Servo Sprint: player manual

**Pick your bot. Outrun the Foreman.**

The Foreman runs the line, and any robot that slows down gets scrapped. Build your bot, paint it your way, and
run.

Servo Sprint is a 3D robot runner. You play as your favourite robot, sprinting down Line 3 with the Foreman
behind you. Everyone starts with **Unit 07**; six more robots wait in the Garage showroom. You dodge crates,
robot arms and rovers, grab gears and tools, and see how far you get before the line catches you. Every run
is new, except the **Daily Run**, which is the same course for everyone that day.

It's free to play. It runs in your browser at **https://servosprint.xyz** on a phone, tablet or computer, and
you can install it like an app. There's nothing to buy with real money. The website may show ads from Google; any
ad that gives you a reward is always optional (see [Privacy](#privacy)).

> This manual describes the current build. Whenever the game changes, this file changes in the same update.

## Quick start

1. Open **servosprint.xyz** and pick a name (3 to 16 letters, numbers or `_`). Your name shows on the leaderboard.
2. Press **Start** (or Space, Enter, or A on a controller). A short 3, 2, 1 countdown runs, then you're off.
3. Change lanes, jump and slide to stay alive. The line speeds up the longer you last.
4. When you crash, you see your score, your best and your rank. Press **Run again**, or tap anywhere on the
   game-over screen outside the buttons.

On your first run the game starts a bit slower and shows short tips the first time you meet something new.
Your first three missions ("Jump 3 times", "Slide twice", "Grab 10 gears in one run") are made to finish on
that first run, so you see gears land in your Toolbox and your first grade come up straight away.

## Controls

There are three lanes. You always move forward, so you only steer, jump and slide.

### Keyboard

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Change lane | **←** **→** or **A** **D** |
| Jump | **↑**, **W** or **Space** |
| Slide | **↓** or **S** |
| Pause / resume | **P** or **Esc** (Space also resumes) |
| Start, or run again after a crash | **Space** or **Enter** |
| Music on/off | **M** |
| Move around menus | **Tab**; leaderboard tabs use the **arrow keys**, **Home** and **End** |

Space and Enter on the menu start whatever the main button shows: a normal run, the Daily Run, or a
friend's challenge.

### Touch (phones and tablets)

| Action | Gesture |
| --- | --- |
| Change lane | Swipe left or right |
| Jump | Swipe up |
| Slide | Swipe down |
| Pause | The pause button in the corner |

Swipes fire as soon as your finger has moved about 10 px, so short flicks work. A thumb swipe that drifts
sideways still counts as up or down: it only changes lane when it's clearly sideways. A swipe you start in the
last beat of the countdown still counts once the run begins.

Prefer buttons? Turn on the **Control wheel** in Settings: a round pad with four quarters (left, right,
jump, slide). **Wheel on the left** moves it for left thumbs.

### Gamepad

Any controller your browser sees as a standard gamepad works. You'll see "Controller connected".

| Action | Buttons |
| --- | --- |
| Change lane | D-pad left/right, left stick, or **LB** / **RB** |
| Jump | **A**, D-pad up, or stick up |
| Slide | **B**, D-pad down, or stick down |
| Pause / resume | **Start** |
| Start, or run again | **A** or **Start** |

The stick needs a firm push to move a lane and has to come back most of the way before it moves again, so a
resting stick can't change lanes twice.

### Forgiving inputs

- **Early jumps count.** Press jump up to 0.3 s before you land and you jump again the moment you touch down.
  The same goes for hops in Roller Mode.
- **Late jumps off a rail count.** Jump just after leaving a rail and it still works.
- The game pauses on its own if you switch tabs, lock your phone or rotate the screen.

## Scoring

Your score climbs all the time while you run, and faster when you pick things up or break things.

| Source | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Distance | 0.5 per unit travelled (about 0.6 per metre) |
| Gear | 10 |
| Screw | 25 |
| Drill through a robot arm | 50 |
| Hammer through a crate, stack or rover | 75 |

### Multipliers

Multipliers boost everything you earn while they're active. They stack by multiplying and **never go past ×6**.

| Multiplier | How you get it | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Golden torque wrench** | Pick up the gold wrench | Starts at **×2**, climbs one step a second to **×3**, **×4**, **×5**, for 10 s. Any contact ends it early. |
| **Overdrive** | Fill the toolbox meter | **×2** for 8 s |
| **Drift combo** (Roller Mode) | Chain lane changes while rolling | A bonus of 20 %, 40 % or 60 % on what you earned during the combo, paid out when it ends |

Golden ×5 with Overdrive would be ×10, so the cap holds it at ×6.

Each step up the golden ladder pops over your robot with a chime (and a light buzz on phones). A clean finish
pops **×1**.

### Your best, and passing it

When you pass your personal best mid-run you get a "New best" moment, and a golden wrench shows up about
2.5 s later. Your best counts across every mode.

### Score limits

There's a ceiling on how fast points can come in (no more than 1,200 in any one second, and a limit based on
how long the run lasted). A perfect run never gets near it. It's there so the leaderboard stays honest.

## Gears, screws and the Toolbox

- **Gears** are worth 10 points and are also your currency. Every gear you collect, plus mission rewards,
  goes into your **Toolbox**.
- **Screws** are worth 25 points. They're for score only and don't go in the Toolbox.
- The **Toolbox** shows your balance on the title screen, in the Garage and at game over. At game over you'll
  see your gears fly into it and the total tick up, with "+N this run" (mission rewards included).
- You spend Toolbox gears in the [Garage](#garage-robots-paint-and-design). Spending never touches your score.

Your balance is simply gears earned minus gears spent, so older saves carried over exactly.

## Power-ups

Tools appear in open lanes after the first 10 seconds of a run. Your **toolbox meter** has five segments:
each tool you grab fills one, and your first smash with a drill or hammer fills half a segment. Fill all
five and you get **Overdrive**.

| Power-up | Looks like | Lasts | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wrench magnet** | Wrench | 6 s | Pulls gears and screws to you from every lane, a short way ahead |
| **Drill** | Drill | 5 s | Bore straight through robot arms (+50 each) |
| **Hammer** | Hammer | 6 s | Smash crates, stacks and rovers (+75 each). The world holds for a split second on each hit. Machines of the line can't be smashed |
| **Golden torque wrench** | Gold wrench | 10 s | ×2 rising to ×5 while you stay clean (see [Multipliers](#multipliers)). The next one comes 60 to 120 s later, or 2.5 s after you pass your best |
| **Overdrive** | Full toolbox meter | 8 s | ×2 on everything |
| **Wheel token → Roller Mode** | Wheel | 8 s | You turn into the UL-W1 roller (see below) |
| **Spare Servo** | Bought in the Garage | One use | A second chance when you crash (see below) |

> Smashing with the hammer or drill counts as contact, so it ends a golden multiplier. Pick your moment.

### Roller Mode

Grab a wheel token (from about 14 seconds in) and your robot folds into a fast roller for 8 seconds. It moves
20 % faster and plays differently:

- **Rails:** roll onto a rail to grind along it, gears lined up on top.
- **Ramps:** hit a ramp to launch through a trail of gears. You can't be hurt in the air or for a moment after you land.
- **Drifts:** change lanes in quick succession to build a drift combo (up to three levels).
- **No slide**, just a low **hop** (swipe up, ↑ or Space). There are no robot arms while you're rolling.

You can't be hurt while the robot transforms, and you blink for a short grace period after you change back.

### Spare Servo

A Spare Servo is a one-time reboot for when a run goes wrong.

- **Price:** 450 gears from your Toolbox (about two or three good runs). You can hold up to **3**.
- **Arm it** in the Garage with "Arm for my next runs". Your first purchase arms it for you. A small cyan servo icon
  on the HUD means it's armed.
- **When you crash**, the robot reboots on the spot, the obstacles just ahead are cleared, and you get **1.5 s** of
  invulnerability (glowing cyan rings). One servo is used up right then.
- **One per run**, and **never in the Daily Run**, so the Daily stays the same test for everyone.
- **Titan** starts every run (not the Daily Run) with its own free spare. It's used before yours, and it's still
  one revive per run, so the servos in your Toolbox stay put.
- Runs that used a servo still count on the all-time and weekly boards, and are marked as revive runs.
- No real money is involved. Servos are only bought with gears.

## Obstacles

The line throws five kinds of hazard at you. You'll never find all three lanes blocked, and pickups only
appear in lanes you can actually reach.

| Obstacle | How to get past | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Crate** | Jump over it, or change lanes | The hammer smashes it |
| **Crate stack** | Change lanes | Two crates high: too tall to jump. The hammer smashes it |
| **Robot arm** | Slide under the laser line, or change lanes | The drill bores through it |
| **Rover** | Jump over it, or change lanes | A low mobile robot driving toward you, from about 20 s in. The hammer smashes it |
| **Gap in the line** | Jump it, or change lanes | Fall in and the run ends |

On your very first run, rows have just one obstacle. As the line speeds up, more rows block two lanes, and
crate stacks join in once you're past the opening stretch. Speed builds steadily for about the first 80 seconds, then holds at the top speed.

When you crash, the game-over headline says what got you and where in the factory: the weld cell, the laser
cutters, the parts racks, the loading dock and so on.

### Factory zones and turns

The line runs through five factory zones, one after another, about 935 m each (roughly a minute early in a
run, half that at top speed). Each has its own light and its own favourite hazard:

| Zone | Light | What it throws at you | Turns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bakery Line** | warm off-white haze | low crates, gaps | tight, winding |
| **Auto Plant** | yellow work lights | welding arms (slide) | long, sweeping |
| **Chip Fab** | clean cyan-white | crate stacks: precise lane switches | medium |
| **Night Port** | dark, cyan rim light | rovers (forklifts) | medium, with climbs and drops |
| **Rocket Factory** | bright off-white | gaps and everything else | medium, climbing the gantry |

Every zone runs the same rhythm: it opens on a straight with roomy rows of its signature hazard on its own,
then mixes it with the rest, and in its last stretch adds more two-lane rows and an S-bend. The zone's name
shows for a moment as you cross into it, and the game-over screen says which zone you reached.

The line steers itself through the turns: you never steer, lanes stay lanes, and every jump, slide and lane
change works exactly as on a straight. The robot leans into a bend (more at speed) and the camera looks
into it, so you see the next hazard coming round the corner.

Free runs and challenges start on the Bakery Line. The **Daily Run** starts in a zone picked from the day's
course, so the starting zone changes from day to day (two days in a row can share one).
Zones and turns come from the course's seed, so a challenge or a Daily Run has the same zones, turns and
hazards for everyone.

### Machines of the line (course v3)

Each zone has its own machine. You meet it alone first on a roomy stretch, then mixed with the zone's obstacles, then in a harder form near the end of the zone.

| Zone | Machine | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bakery Line | Oven door | Yellow lamp: the shutter drops shut just before you get there. Change lanes. Cyan lamp: it stays open. |
| Auto Plant | Stamping press | Keeps a steady beat. The lamp blinks yellow for 0.6 s before the ram slams. Gears sit under it: time it, or slide under a ram that is still rising. Late in the zone, two presses work on opposite beats. |
| Chip Fab | Wafer conveyor | Cyan chevrons push you one lane the way they point. Jump over it to stay in your lane. |
| Night Port | Gantry crane | A container hangs over a marked bay. The marking turns yellow, then the container drops ahead of you. Jump it like a crate or go round. |
| Rocket Factory | Engine test fire | A wall engine glows for 0.7 s, then fires across one lane (two lanes later in the zone). The yellow line on the floor shows how far it reaches. |

**Machines can't be smashed.** The hammer and the drill only work on crates, stacks, rovers and robot arms; against a door, press, conveyor, crane or test fire they do nothing, so read the lamp and pick your lane. Invulnerable moments (a morph, ramp flight, the landing roll, a Spare Servo reboot) still carry you through, and a Spare Servo reboot clears machines just ahead.

**Close shave:** get through a press or past a test fire within a quarter of a second of it going off and you earn +40 and a notch on the Overdrive meter.

### Tricks (course v3)

- **Ramp tricks:** in the air off a Roller Mode ramp, jump to front flip or slide to spin. You can queue one more trick while one is turning. Land square for Flip +120 or Spin +90, and every extra trick in the same jump adds half again. If you land mid-turn, that's Sloppy: no bonus, but no crash either.
- **Grind lines:** a rail pays 3 points per metre when you leave it. There's no balance to keep. Hop onto the next rail within 0.7 s and the line carries on at ×1.5, then ×2.
- All of these count toward the normal score limits.

### The Foreman

The Foreman runs Line 3, and any robot that slows down gets scrapped. Whichever robot you run as, he is never far behind. His drone hangs over your shoulder with a gold
searchlight on your lane.

- **He closes in** as the run goes on and every time you let a gear go by. A Spare Servo reboot brings him
  right up ("Saw that.").
- **He backs off** when you play clean: every hazard you clear in its own lane, every gear, screw and tool
  pushes him back out of view ("Fine. Carry on.").
- **Lane check.** A few times a run (about 25 s, 70 s and 2 minutes in) he overtakes you and holds his light
  on the next real hazard coming at you, then drops back. Use it: the lit lane is the one with trouble in it.
- When you crash, he is there to say so.

His lines show under the score for a couple of seconds (screen readers hear them too). He is a presence,
not a hazard: he never blocks a lane, never changes your score and is the same in the Daily Run for everyone.

## Daily Run and streaks

The **Daily Run** is one course a day, the same for every player.

- A new course opens at **midnight Pacific time** (Vancouver). The Daily card shows today's date and the menu
  shows when the next course opens. So does the game-over screen after a Daily Run.
- Play it as many times as you like. The **Daily** board shows everyone's best run of the day, Daily Run or
  any other run, so a Daily Run score lands there next to free runs and challenges.
- **No Spare Servo** in the Daily Run.
- Open it straight from **https://servosprint.xyz/?daily=1**, or from the "Daily Run" shortcut on the
  installed app's icon.

### Today's twist

Each day also has one **twist**: an extra goal for that day's Daily Run, such as "Grab 20 screws" or
"Last 60 s". It's picked from the date, so everyone gets the same one. Meet it in a Daily Run and you get
**+50 gears**, once a day. The twist only changes the goal, never the course, so the Daily Run itself stays
the same for everyone.

### Ghost racing

On the Daily Run and on challenge courses you race a **ghost**: a see-through cyan copy of your own best run
on that same course. Free runs get a fresh course every time, so they have no ghost.

- Your first run on a course records the ghost. Beat that score and your new run becomes the ghost.
- The chip under the score says how you stand at the same second of the run: **+240** means you're 240 points
  ahead, **−90** behind. It turns cyan while you lead.
- Pull ahead after trailing and you get an **Overtake**: a callout, a chime and a short buzz.
- When the ghost's run ends and yours doesn't, it fades out ("Ghost down") and the chip counts the metres
  you've gone past it.
- The game-over screen says "Ghost beaten" or "Ghost held on".
- The ghost fades when it would sit on top of your robot, so it never hides what's in front of you.
- **Challenger ghost:** when you send someone a challenge (Share or Challenge a friend), your run's ghost goes
  with it. When they open the link they race *your* ghost, labelled with your name, until they beat your score
  on that course; after that they race their own best there. A long name is shortened with "…" so the chip
  stays on one line on small phones.
- Turn it off in **Settings → Ghost racer** (this also stops your ghost being attached to challenges you
  send). Your own ghosts are stored on this device only (today's Daily plus your four most recent courses).
  Ghosts never change the course or anyone's score.
- A ghost belongs to one course: the same seed **and** the same course version. A classic challenge link
  (made before Factory Zones, straight hall) only ever races a classic ghost, and a zones-and-turns course only
  a ghost recorded on it.

### Streaks

Play the Daily Run on consecutive days to build a streak. You get **one free miss a week**: skip a single day
and the streak survives, as long as you haven't used the week's free miss already. Weeks start on Monday.

## Missions

You always have **three missions** on the go, each a different kind (a skill, a distance, a pickup or a style
goal). Each shows its progress in numbers ("640 / 1,000 m"). Finish one and you earn gears for your Toolbox,
and a new mission takes its place. Progress carries across runs unless the mission says "in one run".

- **Swap:** once a day (midnight Pacific) you can swap one mission for a new one with the arrows button
  next to it. The new one starts from zero.
- **Weekly goal:** finish **8 missions** between Monday and Sunday for **+150 gears**, once a week.
- **First missions:** a new player starts with three easy ones (Jump 3 times, Slide twice, Grab 15 gears in
  one run: 20, 20 and 30 gears). They can't be swapped and don't come back.

| Mission | Kind | Reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jump 30 times | Skill | 40 gears |
| Slide 15 times | Skill | 40 gears |
| Score 1,500 in one run | Skill | 70 gears |
| Last 75 s in one run | Skill | 70 gears |
| Run today's Daily Run | Distance | 40 gears |
| Run 1 km in one run | Distance | 70 gears |
| Cover 4 km in total | Distance | 60 gears |
| Collect 50 screws | Pickup | 50 gears |
| Grab 20 screws in one run | Pickup | 60 gears |
| Grab 60 gears in one run | Pickup | 60 gears |
| Pick up 5 tools | Pickup | 50 gears |
| Smash 10 crates or robots | Style | 60 gears |
| Hold the golden wrench 8 s | Style | 80 gears |
| Fill the toolbox for Overdrive | Style | 70 gears |
| Reach a ×3 multiplier | Style | 80 gears |
| Score 600 without a tool | Style | 90 gears |

### Grade

Your **grade** goes up with every gear you bank (run gears, mission and goal rewards). Grade 2 takes 100 gears,
grade 3 300, grade 4 600, grade 5 1,000, and each step after is a little longer (grade 10 at 4,500). Each new
grade pays **10 × the grade** in gears (grade 2: +20, grade 5: +50) and is called out on the game-over screen.
Some grades open new Garage options:

| Grade | Unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | Rivets pattern |
| 3 | Off-white plate ink |
| 4 | Gunmetal paint |
| 6 | Pinstripe pattern |

Players from before grades existed start at the grade their gears already reached (without a back-payment).

## Garage: robots, paint and design

Open the **Garage** with its button on the title screen, next to Daily Run under Start (or from game over). Your robot stands in a close-up and turns slowly so you can see
its back plate. The Garage has three tabs: **Robots**, **Paint** and **Workshop**. Every change shows on the
live robot straight away.

### Robots (the showroom)

Use the arrows or the robot list to walk through the showroom. Robots you own show in full colour; locked ones
stand as a dark silhouette with the grade they need and their price. Buying asks first, then the new robot is
selected straight away. **Select** switches between robots you own. Your robot's name (Workshop) stays the
same whichever robot you run as.

| Robot | What it is | Perk | Unlock |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Unit 07** | The starter humanoid | Balanced | Free |
| **Strider** | Tall, thin reverse-knee walker | Jumps 8% higher; lane changes a touch slower | Grade 4 + 1,500 gears |
| **Pup-4** | Small low quadruped with a sensor-bar head | Quicker hops (same height, about 5% less hang time); up from a slide 0.08 s sooner | Grade 5 + 2,500 gears |
| **Rover** | Squat body on one drive wheel, balancing tail | Snappiest lane change (about 12% quicker); lowest slide; jump 9% lower | Grade 7 + 3,500 gears |
| **Mag-Bot** | Round chassis with a magnetic coil on its back | Pulls in gears up to 3 units ahead in its lane and the next (not screws); jump 3% lower | Grade 10 + 6,000 gears |
| **Hauler** | Big flat-backed cargo quadruped with a crate rack | Ploughs through one small crate per run without a crash or a servo (no points; it ends a golden multiplier like any contact); jump 3% lower, lanes a touch slower | Grade 12 + 8,000 gears |
| **Titan** | Full humanoid frame with exposed actuators | A free Spare Servo every run; jump 4% lower, lanes a touch slower | Grade 14 + 10,000 gears |

**Fair play:** every robot has exactly the same hitbox as Unit 07, perks are small and each has a cost, and in
the **Daily Run every robot runs on Unit 07's numbers with no perk**, so the shared course stays equal. Score
limits are the same for everyone.

**Test drive:** once a day (Pacific time) you can take any robot you don't own for one free run. It's a normal
free run: the score goes on the boards and the gears go in your Toolbox. The next run is back in your own robot.

### Paint

Paint your robot's **body** and **accent** (the darker panels and joints). Each robot keeps its own paint.

| Kind | Choices | Price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid (body or accent) | Off-white, Graphite, Signal yellow, Cyan, Gunmetal (grade 4), Periwinkle, Navy, Slate, Sage, Mint, Lilac, Rose | Free on any robot you own |
| Sheen (solid body) | Gloss, Satin, Matte | Free |
| Metallic (body or accent) | Gunmetal metallic, Cobalt, Emerald, Crimson | 400 gears each |
| Rare finish (body) | Brushed titanium 1,500 · Carbon fibre 2,000 · Anodized aurora 3,000 · Polished chrome 4,000 · Prototype gold (grade 15) 8,000 | |

Rare finishes are real materials, not just a colour: brushed titanium has a fine grain, carbon fibre a woven
twill, anodized aurora shifts from teal to violet as it turns, and chrome and gold reflect the factory around
them. Tap anything you don't own to **try it on**: the turntable shows it, and the Garage asks before buying
(or tap **Back to mine**). Metallics and finishes are bought once and work on every robot you own. Paint you
already had (and its gloss, satin or matte) carries over to Unit 07.

Name and plate, plate pattern and ink, the Spare Servo, trims, trails and hammer skins.

### Name and plate

| Setting | Rules |

| **Plate text** | Up to 8 characters: A to Z, 0 to 9 and `-`, in capitals (for example `SS-07`) |

Your robot's name shows under your name on the leaderboard, on the title screen ("*Torque Queen* is ready")
and on your share card. With no name, the title screen and share card use the robot's own name ("Strider is
ready").

### Badges (from the boards)

Badges are earned on the boards and checked by the game's server, so they can't be bought or unlocked on your device.
They sit on a shelf at the top of the Garage's **Workshop** tab, with the date you earned each one and a count for the
ones you can earn again. When a run earns one, the game-over screen says **New badge**, and your run's share card shows
up to three of them.

| Badge | How it's earned |
| --- | --- |
| Daily Champion | Finish a day at #1 on the Daily board (counted again each day you do) |
| Beat the Dev | Beat the developer's best run at the moment you post it, without a Spare Servo |
| Crew Winner | Win a Crew that at least three saved names raced in (counted for every Crew you win) |
| 10k Club | Score 10,000 in one run, without a Spare Servo |
| Daily Top 10 | Finish a day in the Daily board's top 10 (counted each day) |
| Weekly Top 10 | Finish a week in the weekly board's top 10 (counted each week) |
| 30-Day Shift | Play the Daily Run 30 Vancouver days in a row (no skipped days) |
| Crew Founder | Start a Crew that three saved names race in |
| Early Shift | Your name was saved before badges opened, and you've played at least 10 runs on 3 different days |

Day and week badges arrive a few hours after the day or week ends (a run that started before midnight can still count),
and Crew badges a few hours after the Crew closes. Badges are held against your name: save your name to keep them.
They are just badges, with no money value, and the game can take one away if a run turns out to be cheated.

### Friends, Crews and streaks

These rewards are paint jobs, badges and gears. None of them adds points to a run: the boards stay pure skill.

| Badge | How it's earned |
| --- | --- |
| Rival (3 levels) | Challenge a friend. Each friend with a saved name who races your link counts: 1, 5 and 25 friends |

## Your name, passkeys and backup codes

You don't need an email or password. Your name and best score live on your device. To keep them across
devices, and to stop anyone else posting under your name, **save** your name.

### Save with a passkey (recommended)

A passkey uses your phone or computer's own unlock: Face ID, Touch ID, an Android fingerprint or Windows Hello.
Your password manager (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager) syncs it to your other devices, so you can sign
in there with one tap.

- Choose **Save across devices** and follow your device's prompt.
- On a new device, choose sign in and pick your passkey. You don't even need to type your name.
- Passkeys work on servosprint.xyz in browsers that support "related origins": the game asks the browser
  directly, and also accepts Chrome and Edge 128 or newer. Recent Safari (18 and later) reports support too.
  If your browser doesn't, the game won't offer a passkey and the backup code works instead.

### Backup codes

A **backup code** is 10 letters and numbers. It's shown **once**, so write it down or keep it in your password
manager, then press "I've saved it".

- A new name saved with a passkey also gets a backup code.
- **Load a backup code / Use a backup code:** enter your name and the code on any device to bring your save over.
- **Get a backup code** (when signed in with a passkey) makes a fresh one. The old code stops working.
- After **10 wrong codes in a minute** for one name, that name has to wait a minute ("Too many tries. Give it
  a minute."). It lifts on its own.

### What syncs

Best score, number of runs, gears, Toolbox spending, Garage items, your robot's design and your Spare Servos.
Your grade follows your gears, so it syncs too. Missions, swaps, the weekly goal, the Daily twist and the free
test drive are kept on each device.

**Your robots follow your save.** Once your name is saved (passkey or backup code), the robots, metallics and
finishes you buy, each robot's paint and the robot you picked come with you: sign in or load your backup code on
another device and they are there, with the same Toolbox balance. Robots you bought before this update move up
by themselves the first time that device syncs, and the gears they cost are not taken again.

- **Offline is fine.** Buy, paint and pick while offline; it all goes up the next time the game reaches the
  server.
- **Two devices merge safely.** Scores and gears only move up, a robot or finish bought on either device is kept
  on both, and the robot you picked (or painted) most recently wins.
- **Not saved yet?** Your robots stay on this device until you save your name.

### Sign out and switch player

Tap your name on the title screen ("Welcome back, **name** ⌄") for **Switch player** and **Sign out**. Settings on
the title screen has **Sign out of name** too, and **Switch player** sits under the menu. Each one asks first:

- **Saved name** (passkey or backup code): the game sends anything the server hasn't got yet (a held run, a sync),
  then signs out. Sign back in on this or any device with **Sign in with passkey** or your name and backup code.
- **Not saved yet:** the dialog warns that signing out **deletes this name's progress on this device** (best,
  gears, robots, paint, missions, streak) and can't be undone. **Save first** opens the usual passkey / backup code
  save; **Sign out anyway** really does delete it.
- **Saved, but this device has progress the server hasn't got** (you're offline, a run hasn't reached the board, or
  the last sync failed): the dialog says so. **Sync first** sends it (when you're online); **Sign out anyway**
  drops what didn't reach the server.

Signing out forgets the player on this device: the name, passkey session, backup code, held run, robots and paint,
missions, streak, Daily best, crews you joined here and your ghost tracks. Your device settings stay: sound and
music, volumes, Reduce motion, the control wheel, vibration and the ghost racer switch. Then you're back at the name
picker. **Switch player** does the same and asks "Who's playing?".

Other tabs of the game on the same device follow: an idle tab goes to the name picker ("Signed out in another
tab"); a tab mid-run finishes the run without saving it and goes to the picker after.

## Sharing and challenges

After a run you can:

- **Challenge a friend** (the cyan button right under your score): sends a link to the exact course you just ran,
  with a short message ("Beat my 7,945 in Servo Sprint. Same course, and my ghost races you."). On iPhone and Android
  the share sheet opens; on a computer the message and link are copied, and the button turns into **Link copied**.
  When your friend's chat app shows the link, its preview card shows your name, the score to beat and your robot in
  its paint (plus your rank, when that score is your best on the board).
- **Share card**: a 1080 x 1350 image of your run: the score, a "Beat my N" line, your robot in its paint, your rank
  if you're on the board, and a **Race my ghost** call with the address. The share text names your robot ("Strider
  outran the Foreman for 2,140 in Servo Sprint. Beat my 2,140:"). On iPhone and Android the share sheet opens. On a
  computer the card downloads and the link is copied for you.

Daily Runs and beaten challenges get their own wording and card line, and Daily cards show the date.

### The share sheet

Every share in the game opens the same sheet: after a run, from a crew, from the title screen, for your Daily rank
and from the Garage.

- **Post 4:5 / Story 9:16:** pick the card size. Post is 1080 x 1350 (feeds); Story is 1080 x 1920 (Instagram, TikTok
  and Snapchat stories).
- **Share...** (phones): your phone's own share sheet, with the card as an image plus the text and link. This is how
  the card reaches Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat and WhatsApp, which have no web "post" link.
- **X, WhatsApp, Facebook, Reddit, Telegram:** open that site's share page in a new tab with the text and link filled in.
- **Copy link** and **Save image** (the card as a PNG). On a computer, save the image to post it on Instagram or TikTok.

From the title screen: **Start a crew**, **Dare my N** once you have a best score, and **Share my #N** when you're
in a top 10. **Dare my N** uses your best score even when you set it on another device: if this device ran that
course, or the board knows its course (its ghost is stored), the link is a challenge on that course; if not, it
shares your best score with a link to the game. The Daily card offers to share today's rank.

### Crews

A **crew** is a race for a group on one course. Start one from the title screen (**Start a crew**), or after a run
with **Crew on this course**. You get a link (servosprint.xyz/r/CODE) to send anywhere.

- A crew runs for **24 hours**, for up to 50 robots. Each player's **best** run counts; run it as often as you like.
- Opening the link shows the crew: who started it, the time left and the standings. **Join the race** runs the
  crew's course, and the leader's ghost races you.
- After a crew run you see where you stand ("You took the lead · 3 robots", "You're #2 of 3 · 200 behind sam_p")
  and a one-tap re-share (**I took the lead**). **Run it back** runs the crew's course again.
- Next time you open the game, a banner tells you if someone took your lead, or when a crew you're in has ended.
- When it ends, the crew shows a **podium** with the final standings, and you can **Start a rematch**.
- Crew runs count on the Daily, weekly and all-time boards like any other run.
- Starting lots of crews in a row is limited ("That's a lot of crews. Give it a minute.").

### Opening a challenge

A challenge link opens on one screen: **"<name> challenged you"**, the score to beat in big cyan numbers, their
robot in its paint, and one **Play** button (new players type a racer name just above it). It's the same course, and
their ghost runs beside you. Links made before factory zones existed (no `v=2` in the link) still open the exact
course your friend ran: the classic straight hall, and the challenge screen says so. Beat the score and you'll see
"Challenge beaten". Daily links read **Start the Daily Run**.

Links to the game's old address forward to servosprint.xyz with the challenge intact. Challenge links made before
crews work exactly as before.

## FAQ and troubleshooting

**There's no sound.** Browsers only start sound after your first tap or key press, so tap once. On iPhone the
ringer switch also mutes the game. Press **M**, or check Music and Sound effects in Settings.

**It's choppy on my phone.** The game lowers its own detail if it notices sustained slow frames. Close other
tabs and apps, and try turning off Low Power Mode.

**My score didn't post.** If you were offline or the board was busy, your best run is held on your device and
posts after your next run. Runs shorter than 3 seconds don't post.

**"That name is already saved by someone."** Someone else has saved that name. If it's yours, sign in with your
passkey or backup code. Otherwise pick another.

**"Too many tries. Give it a minute."** Ten wrong backup codes in a minute lock that name for the rest of the
minute. Wait, then try again with the right code.

**I lost my backup code.** Sign in with your passkey and choose **Get a backup code**. Without a passkey or a
code, a saved name can't be recovered.

**Why can't I use a Spare Servo in the Daily Run?** So the Daily is the same test for everyone.

**Do Spare Servo runs count on the board?** Yes, on the weekly and all-time boards, and they aren't marked
there. The limits on how many points a run can score are the same either way. Your own game-over screen says
"Spare Servo used" when you used one.

**Do I lose my gears if I clear my browser?** Your save lives in the browser. Save your name with a passkey or
backup code first and you can bring it back.

**Does my save from the game's old address carry over?** Not automatically yet. Old links now open servosprint.xyz,
which keeps its own save, so you start fresh there. A one-time import of the old save is planned. If your name was
saved with a passkey or backup code, sign in with it and your synced stats come back.

**How do I sign out?** Tap your name on the title screen, then **Sign out** (or Settings, **Sign out of name**). If
your name isn't saved yet, the game warns you first and offers **Save first**, because signing out deletes an
unsaved name's progress on that device. A saved name signs back in with your passkey or backup code. See
[Sign out and switch player](#sign-out-and-switch-player).

**Someone else wants to play on my phone.** Tap your name, then **Switch player**. Save your own name first so you
can sign back in.

**There's no "Dare my N" on my phone, but my best is from my computer.** Fixed in r17c: the dare follows your synced
best. You may see it link to the game rather than the course, when this phone can't learn that run's course.

**Is it in landscape too?** Yes. Rotating pauses the run so you don't lose it.

## Privacy

*Last updated: 2026-10-22.*

The full privacy policy is at **[servosprint.xyz/privacy](https://servosprint.xyz/privacy)** (also linked in game:
Settings > Privacy). It covers who runs the game, IP addresses, how long things are kept and how to ask for deletion
(support@servosprint.xyz). In short:

- **No email, password or tracking pixels, and no cookies from the game.** The game has no analytics of its own;
  the host, Cloudflare, counts page views without cookies (below).
- **On your device:** your name, scores, settings, Toolbox, Garage design, and your save code or sign-in
  token are kept in your browser's local storage.
- **On the server (if you play online):** your name, your scores with their run length, mode and date, your
  robot's name and design, and whether a run used a Spare Servo. When you share a challenge, set a new Crew best or a
  new personal best (with Ghost racer on): that run's ghost (where your robot was each tenth of a second, and its score), with no name attached, deleted after 60 days. If you save your name: a scrambled (hashed)
  copy of your backup code, your passkey's public key (never your face or fingerprint, which stay on your
  device), and your synced stats, Garage items and robots (which robots and finishes you own, their paint and the
  robot you picked). Badges you earn are stored against your name with the date (no age, no other details).
- **Page views:** the game doesn't send a page-view ping of its own. Cloudflare Web Analytics is on for
  servosprint.xyz: it counts page views and page speed without cookies, and isn't loaded for visitors who reach
  Cloudflare through its data centres in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland.
- **Error reports:** if the game hits an error it sends the error message and build number, with no personal
  details and with web addresses trimmed of their query strings.
- **Ads:** servosprint.xyz may show ads through Google AdSense and Google's H5 Games Ads. When it does, Google
  and its partners may use cookies or device identifiers to serve and measure ads. How Google uses this
  information: [How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).
  Players in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland are asked for consent through a Google-certified consent management
  platform (CMP) before personalized ads are shown. Rewarded ads (watch an ad for a bonus, such as extra gears) are
  always optional: you choose whether to watch one, and you never need to watch an ad to play.
- **Third parties:** the 3D engine (three.js) loads from the jsDelivr CDN. Fonts, music and art are all served
  from the game itself (the music track is CC0, by Zane Little Music; see Credits). Ads, if shown, come from
  Google (see above).
